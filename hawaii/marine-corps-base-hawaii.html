Medicare near Marine Corps Base Hawaii (Kaneohe Bay)

Why the Medicare choice is different for a Kaneohe Bay family

Windward Oahu’s military retirees reach 65 with TRICARE For Life, which requires Medicare Parts A and B, pays secondary to Medicare and carries creditable pharmacy coverage. A Medigap policy and a Part D plan are usually redundant for a TFL family; the open question is whether an MA-only Advantage plan’s extras are worth it, and whether its network includes Adventist Health Castle in Kailua and the Honolulu hospitals over the Pali.

Tripler sees retirees on a space-available basis; Medicare does not pay there. Veterans without TFL use the Matsunaga VA Medical Center on the Tripler campus, and for them enrolling in Part B on time is the decision that matters, because VA care is not creditable for Part B.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. Moving to another island or losing a plan opens a Special Enrollment Period.

Questions from Marine Corps Base Hawaii (Kaneohe Bay) families

Frequently asked questions

I retired from the Marines with TRICARE For Life. Do I need anything besides Medicare A and B?

Usually not. TFL fills Medicare’s gaps and its pharmacy is creditable. An MA-only Advantage plan can add dental or vision; we check whether one is worth it and whether its network reaches Castle and your Honolulu doctors.

Does Medicare pay at Tripler?

No. Tripler sees retirees on a space-available basis under TRICARE rules. Most Kaneohe Bay retirees pair TFL with civilian care at Castle, Queen’s or Kaiser.

I use the Matsunaga VA. Do I need Part B?

In most cases, yes. VA care is not creditable for Part B; delaying it triggers a permanent penalty.

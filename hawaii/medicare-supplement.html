Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plans in Hawaii

A Medigap policy works alongside Original Medicare to pay much of what it leaves to you, and lets you use any provider that accepts Medicare — Queen’s, Hilo Benioff, Wilcox, or a specialist on the mainland — with no island lines.

At a glance

  • Hawaii uses the federal standardized plans, sold by letter (Plan G and Plan N are the most common for people new to Medicare; Plan F is closed to anyone eligible after 2019). The same letter offers the same benefits from every company, so the comparison is price and rate history. The Hawaii Insurance Division (DCCA) regulates the policies.
  • Your six-month Medigap open enrollment starts when you are enrolled in Part B — and in Hawaii it does not matter how old you are. State law requires every Medigap insurer to offer every plan, guaranteed issue and without health-based pricing, during that window, and people under 65 pay the same rates as people at 65.
  • Hawaii has no birthday rule, anniversary rule or annual switching window. Outside your open enrollment and guaranteed-issue events, insurers can use medical underwriting.
  • Medigap does not pay for care inside Kaiser Permanente. If you want Kaiser doctors, you want Kaiser Senior Advantage, not a supplement. Everyone else — Queen’s, Hawaii Pacific Health, the public hospitals, mainland referrals — is covered by any Medigap policy without a network.
  • Losing an Advantage plan or employer coverage through no fault of your own creates a guaranteed-issue right, generally 63 days long.

Medicare Supplement insurance — usually called Medigap — is private coverage that pairs with Original Medicare (Parts A and B). Instead of replacing Medicare, it fills the gaps: the deductibles, copayments and coinsurance you’d otherwise pay yourself. You then add a standalone Part D drug plan for prescriptions. The Hawaii Insurance Division of the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs regulates the policies; the benefits inside each plan letter are set federally.

The plans people in Hawaii actually buy

Plan G covers everything Original Medicare leaves behind except the Part B deductible ($283 in 2026). Plan N costs less in exchange for small office and emergency copays and no coverage of Part B excess charges. High-deductible Plan G has a much lower premium and a deductible you pay first. Plan F still exists for people who were eligible before 2020 but cannot be sold to anyone newer. Because benefits are standardized, we compare companies on price and on how fast they have raised it.

When you can buy one without health questions

  • Your six-month open enrollment, at any age. It starts the month you are enrolled in Part B. Federal law guarantees that window only at 65 and older; Hawaii law extends it to everyone, so a person under 65 on Medicare because of a disability gets every plan from every insurer, guaranteed issue, at the same rates as a 65-year-old. A second open enrollment arrives at 65.
  • Guaranteed-issue events. Losing an Advantage plan or employer coverage through no fault of your own gives you a window (generally 63 days) to buy certain plans without underwriting.
  • Outside those windows, Hawaii insurers can use medical underwriting, and Hawaii has no birthday rule, anniversary rule or annual Medigap window. Switching later usually means answering health questions.

Timing matters. A condition that would be irrelevant at 65 can mean a decline at 72. If you are approaching 65, or your Advantage plan just sent a non-renewal notice, talk to us before the window closes.

Compare the rate history, not just the first-year price

Because the benefits are standardized, the only real differences between Hawaii Medigap companies are what they charge and how steeply they raise it later. That second part is public: every carrier files its rate increases with the Hawaii Insurance Division, and a policy that looks cheap at 65 can be the expensive one by 75. We publish that filing history on our research site, Hawaii Medigap rate history, with each figure tied to the filing it came from. If your premium has already gone up and you want to know why, why Medigap premiums increase covers the three causes.

Who Medigap tends to suit

People who want maximum freedom to choose doctors and hospitals — Queen’s from Molokai, a mainland cancer center from Hilo, Wilcox and Straub in the same year — predictable costs, and coverage that travels. That is why so many neighbor island residents and people who travel for care keep a supplement. The exceptions are Kaiser members, who need Senior Advantage to keep Kaiser, and military retirees with TRICARE For Life, who usually do not need a supplement at all. The cost is a monthly premium that rises with age and with the carrier’s filings.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a separate drug plan with Medigap?

Yes. Medigap does not include prescription coverage, so most people add a standalone Part D plan. We help you pick one around your specific medications.

Can I be turned down for Medigap in Hawaii?

Not during your six-month open enrollment, which in Hawaii applies at any age once you have Part B, and not during a guaranteed-issue event such as your Advantage plan leaving your county. Outside those windows, Hawaii insurers can use medical underwriting, and Hawaii has no birthday rule or annual switching window.

I am under 65 on Medicare because of a disability. Can I buy Medigap in Hawaii?

Yes, on the same terms as someone turning 65. Hawaii requires every Medigap insurer to offer every plan, guaranteed issue and without health-based pricing, during the six months after your Part B begins, and prohibits charging under-65 enrollees more because of their disability. You get a fresh open enrollment at 65.

Does Medigap work at Kaiser?

No. Kaiser Permanente is an integrated system that provides care only to its own plan members, so a Medigap policy has nothing to pay there. If you want to keep Kaiser doctors on Medicare, the answer is Kaiser Senior Advantage.

Is Plan F still available in Hawaii?

Only to people who became eligible for Medicare before January 1, 2020. Everyone newer chooses from Plans G, N, high-deductible G and the others. We walk through which fits you.

How much does a Hawaii Medigap policy cost?

It depends on the plan letter, your age, tobacco use and the carrier, and every carrier raises rates on its own schedule. We compare current premiums and each company’s filed rate history with you; we do not publish a number here without the filing behind it.

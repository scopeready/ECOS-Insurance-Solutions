At a glance

  • 2026 Part B standard premium $202.90/month; Part B deductible $283; Part A hospital deductible $1,736 per benefit period.
  • 2026 Part D: out-of-pocket cap $2,100; maximum deductible $615; national base premium $38.99.
  • IRMAA surcharges begin above $109,000 (single) or $218,000 (joint) of 2024 modified adjusted gross income, and are a cliff: $1 over a threshold moves you to the whole next tier. In Hawaii the usual surprise is the sale of a house or a rental, or a Roth conversion two years ago.
  • State and county retirees on EUTF plans must enroll in Part B and are reimbursed the premium; retirees hired before July 1, 2023 are also reimbursed IRMAA, those hired later are not, and nobody is reimbursed a late penalty.
  • Part B late penalty: 10% for each full 12 months without Part B or creditable coverage, for life. Part D late penalty: about 1% of the base premium per month without creditable drug coverage.

EUTF retirees: Part B is required, and reimbursed

If you retired from the State of Hawaii, the Department of Education, the University of Hawaii or a county, your EUTF retiree medical plan requires you to enroll in Medicare Part B when you become eligible and to send EUTF proof within 60 days, or the retiree plan is cancelled. EUTF then reimburses eligible retirees (and covered spouses) for the Part B premium. Retirees hired before July 1, 2023 are also reimbursed any IRMAA surcharge; retirees hired on or after that date are reimbursed the standard premium only. EUTF does not reimburse late-enrollment penalties, which is one more reason to enroll on time. Details are in the Turning 65 guide.

2026 IRMAA: what higher earners pay

If your income is above the thresholds below, you pay an income-related surcharge on top of your Part B and Part D premiums. Your 2026 IRMAA is based on the income (MAGI) from your 2024 tax return. IRMAA is a cliff: going $1 over a threshold moves you into the whole next tier. In Hawaii the usual surprises are the sale of a house or a rental unit, a year of strong rental income, or a Roth conversion two years back.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard Medicare Part B premium for 2026?

The standard Part B premium is $202.90 per month in 2026, with a $283 annual deductible. Higher-income beneficiaries pay more through IRMAA.

What is IRMAA?

IRMAA (Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount) is a surcharge added to your Part B and Part D premiums if your income is above certain thresholds. For 2026 it is based on your 2024 tax return and starts above $109,000 (single) or $218,000 (joint).

Does EUTF pay my Part B premium?

EUTF reimburses the Part B premium for eligible state and county retirees and their covered spouses who are enrolled in Part B. Retirees hired before July 1, 2023 are also reimbursed IRMAA; those hired later are not. Late-enrollment penalties are never reimbursed.

What is the Part D out-of-pocket cap in 2026?

$2,100. Once your spending on covered drugs reaches that amount in 2026, you pay nothing more for covered medications for the rest of the year.

Can I get help paying the Part B premium in Hawaii?

Possibly. Hawaii’s Medicare Savings Programs (QMB, SLMB and QI), run by Med-QUEST, pay the Part B premium for people with limited income and resources, and QMB also covers Medicare’s deductibles and copays. Apply at mybenefits.hawaii.gov or 1-800-316-8005; see our Med-QUEST page.

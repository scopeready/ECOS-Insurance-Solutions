Medicare in the region

Park City & the Wasatch Back: what shapes the choice here

Park City and the Heber Valley have grown into a retirement region of their own, with Intermountain’s Park City and Heber Valley hospitals for local care and Salt Lake’s specialists forty minutes away. The Medicare question here is often a two-home question, and the answer usually starts with a Medigap policy.

Summit and Wasatch counties’ Advantage menus are moderate and built mostly around Intermountain; we compare them honestly against a supplement with your travel in view.

What to watch: A large share of Wasatch Back residents keep another home elsewhere, and county-based Advantage plans are tied to permanent residence. A Medigap policy travels; an HMO from Summit County covers emergencies only in Scottsdale.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. Moving counties opens a Special Enrollment Period.

Questions from the Wasatch Back

Frequently asked questions

Which Medicare plans are available in Park City?

Summit and Wasatch counties typically offer a moderate Medicare Advantage menu built around Intermountain, every Medigap carrier in Utah, and the full Part D lineup. Availability changes each plan year.

I split the year between Park City and Scottsdale. What works?

A Medigap policy with Original Medicare works in both places. County-based Advantage and Part D plans are tied to your permanent residence, and most Advantage HMOs cover only emergencies out of area. See our snowbird guide.

How much does your help cost?

Nothing. Independent agents are paid by the carriers when you enroll, so comparing plans and reviewing your coverage each year is free to you.

Medicare in the region

Northwest & the Four Corners: what shapes the choice here

The Four Corners is New Mexico’s clearest case of distance shaping the plan choice. San Juan Regional Medical Center in Farmington is the region’s trauma center and the referral point for everything from Shiprock to Aztec; Gallup has Rehoboth McKinley and the IHS’s Gallup Indian Medical Center; Grants has a small hospital; and the reservation chapters between them are an hour or more from any of it. For a cardiologist or an oncologist, most of the region drives to Albuquerque, and many Farmington families drive north to Durango or Denver instead.

Medicare and the Indian Health Service fit together better than most people assume: IHS encourages eligible tribal members to enroll, Medicare pays IHS and tribal facilities for covered care, and it pays for the referrals the IHS cannot fund. Our rural New Mexico guide lays it out with the federal sources.

What to watch: A large share of the region’s Medicare beneficiaries are members of the Navajo Nation, the Pueblos of Zuni, Acoma and Laguna and other tribes whose primary care runs through IHS and tribal facilities. The IHS is not insurance and its referral program is the payer of last resort, so Medicare enrollment matters here, and whether an Advantage plan lists your IHS hospital in its network is the first question to ask. Farmington families also cross into Colorado for care, which a Medigap policy covers and an Advantage network may not.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. A plan leaving your county opens a Special Enrollment Period, and from January 1, 2027, New Mexico adds a 60-day Medigap birthday window.

Questions from the Four Corners

Frequently asked questions

Which Medicare plans are available in Farmington and Gallup?

San Juan and McKinley counties typically offer a moderate Medicare Advantage menu, every Medigap carrier in New Mexico, and the full Part D lineup. Cibola County has fewer Advantage plans. Availability is set by county and changes each plan year.

I get my care at an IHS hospital. Do I still need Medicare?

The IHS says it is not health insurance and that its Purchased/Referred Care program is the payer of last resort, and it encourages eligible tribal members to enroll in Medicare. Enrolling does not affect your IHS eligibility, it pays the facility for your care, and it covers care the facility cannot provide. Enroll in Part B on time.

How much does your help cost?

Nothing. Independent agents are paid by the carriers when you enroll, so comparing plans and reviewing your coverage each year is free to you.

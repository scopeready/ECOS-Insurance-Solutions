Veterans and Medicare in Arizona

VA coverage and Medicare are not substitutes. The mistake is assuming one replaces the other.

Arizona has one of the larger veteran populations in the country, concentrated in the Phoenix and Tucson areas and around Sierra Vista. The question we field most often is whether Medicare is necessary at all when you already have VA health care. Usually the answer is yes.

Where VA coverage stops

VA health care is delivered at VA facilities, by VA providers. It is real coverage and often excellent, but it has boundaries that Medicare does not:

  • Care at a non-VA hospital is generally not covered unless it was authorised in advance or meets specific emergency criteria.
  • Prescriptions filled outside the VA pharmacy system are generally not covered.
  • If you are travelling, or the nearest VA facility is a long drive — a real consideration in northern and western Arizona — VA coverage may not help you where you actually are.

Medicare fills those gaps. Most veterans are best served carrying both.

The Part B decision you cannot undo

This is the part worth being careful about. Declining Part B because you have VA coverage can create a permanent late-enrolment penalty — 10% of the Part B premium for every full 12 months you were eligible and did not enrol, for as long as you have Part B.

VA health care is not considered creditable coverage for the purpose of delaying Part B. Neither is it for Part D. Active employer coverage is; VA coverage is not. That distinction catches people every year.

TRICARE For Life

If you are a military retiree, TRICARE For Life works as secondary coverage behind Medicare — and it requires you to have Part B. Drop Part B and you lose TRICARE For Life. It is a strong combination: Medicare pays first, TRICARE picks up most of the remainder, and TRICARE includes prescription coverage that generally counts as creditable for Part D purposes.

If you have TRICARE For Life, adding a Medicare Advantage plan needs careful thought, because it changes how Medicare pays and therefore how TRICARE coordinates behind it. Worth a conversation before you change anything.

What we would check with you

  • Whether you have VA health care, TRICARE For Life, CHAMPVA, or a combination — they behave differently.
  • How far you are from your VA facility, and whether you use non-VA providers.
  • Where you fill prescriptions, since that drives whether you need Part D.
  • Whether you are still working, which can change the Part B timing.

None of this costs anything to review, and getting it right at the start avoids a penalty that never goes away.

Common questions

I use the VA. Do I still need Medicare?

Usually yes. VA care is delivered at VA facilities and does not cover prescriptions filled outside the VA system or care at non-VA hospitals except in limited circumstances. Most veterans carry both. Declining Part B to rely on VA coverage alone can also create a permanent late-enrolment penalty.

Does VA coverage let me delay Part B without a penalty?

No. VA health care is not treated as creditable coverage for delaying Part B or Part D. Active employer coverage is; VA coverage is not. Delaying on the assumption that it counts is one of the more costly mistakes we see, because the penalty is permanent.

I have TRICARE For Life. Do I need Part B?

Yes. TRICARE For Life requires Medicare Part B — if you drop Part B you lose TRICARE For Life. TRICARE works as secondary coverage behind Medicare and includes prescription coverage that generally counts as creditable for Part D purposes.

Can I have a Medicare Advantage plan and TRICARE For Life?

It is possible but deserves careful review. A Medicare Advantage plan changes how Medicare pays, which changes how TRICARE coordinates behind it. This is one of the situations where a short conversation before enrolling is worth far more than fixing it afterwards.

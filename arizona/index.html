Your Medicare coverage options

There's no one "best" plan — only the best plan for your situation. Here's how the pieces fit.

Original Medicare (Part A & B)

Federal hospital and medical coverage. Part A is premium-free for most people; Part B is $202.90/mo in 2026. It pays 80% after the deductible — there's no out-of-pocket cap on its own.

Medicare Advantage (Part C)

An all-in-one private plan that replaces Original Medicare, often with $0 or low premiums and built-in dental, vision, and drug coverage. Uses networks and copays, with a yearly out-of-pocket maximum.

Medicare Supplement (Medigap)

Pairs with Original Medicare to cover most of the 20% it leaves behind. See any provider that takes Medicare, nationwide. Higher monthly premium, and you add a separate Part D plan.

Part D (Prescription drugs)

Drug coverage, either standalone or built into an Advantage plan. In 2026, your total out-of-pocket for covered drugs is capped at $2,100.

Late enrollment penalty calculators

Missing your enrollment window can mean a lifelong surcharge. Estimate it here.

Part B penalty

Adds 10% to your Part B premium for each full 12 months you were eligible but not enrolled — for as long as you have Part B.

Part D penalty

Adds 1% of the national base premium ($38.99 in 2026) for each full month you went without creditable drug coverage.

Estimates only. Your actual penalty is set by Medicare. The best move is to enroll on time — call me if you're unsure.

Common questions

When can I enroll in Medicare?

Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your 65th birthday (3 months before, your birthday month, and 3 months after). Most people can also make changes during the Annual Enrollment Period, October 15 - December 7 each year. Certain life events open a Special Enrollment Period.

Does it cost more to use a local agent?

No. My help is free to you. Plan premiums are the same whether you enroll through a licensed agent, online, or directly with the carrier — agents are paid by the insurance companies, not by you.

What's the difference between Medicare Advantage and a Medigap plan?

A Medicare Advantage plan is an all-in-one alternative to Original Medicare, often with $202.90-and-up low or $0 premiums and built-in extras like dental and drug coverage, but with networks and copays. A Medigap (supplement) plan pairs with Original Medicare to cover most out-of-pocket costs and lets you see any provider that accepts Medicare, usually for a higher monthly premium plus a separate Part D plan.

Do you charge for a plan review?

Never. I'll review your current coverage, doctors, and prescriptions and compare your options at no cost. You're never obligated to enroll.

Medicare in Gilbert, Arizona

A younger east-valley town where Medicare usually arrives alongside a parent's care question.

Gilbert is one of the younger large towns in Maricopa County. Many of the Medicare conversations we have here are not about the person calling — they are about a parent, and they arrive at a difficult moment.

Helping a parent from Gilbert

Two situations come up most:

  • A parent moving to Arizona to be near you. Relocating out of their old plan's service area triggers a Special Enrolment Period, so they are not stuck until autumn. Their previous Advantage plan will not have a network here; Original Medicare and Medigap travel, Advantage plans do not.
  • A parent entering assisted living or needing long-term care. This is where families discover that Medicare does not pay for long-term custodial care. It covers skilled nursing under specific conditions for a limited period — days 21 to 100 carry $217 per day in 2026 — and not indefinite room and board. ALTCS is the Arizona programme that does, and unlike most states it has no waiting list.

Common questions

My parent is moving to Gilbert. Can they change plans?

Yes. Moving out of a plan's service area triggers a Special Enrolment Period, so they do not have to wait for the Annual Enrolment Period. Their existing Medicare Advantage plan will not have a network in Arizona, so a change is usually necessary rather than optional.

Does Medicare pay for assisted living or a nursing home?

Not for long-term custodial care. Medicare covers skilled nursing under specific conditions and for a limited period, with $217 per day coinsurance for days 21 through 100 in 2026. It does not cover indefinite room and board. In Arizona, ALTCS covers long-term care for those who qualify — and it is an entitlement with no waiting list.

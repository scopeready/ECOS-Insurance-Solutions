Medicare near Seymour Johnson AFB (Goldsboro)

Why the Medicare choice is different for a Seymour Johnson family

Wayne County’s Air Force retirees reach 65 with TRICARE For Life, which requires Medicare Parts A and B, pays secondary to Medicare and carries creditable pharmacy coverage. A Medigap policy and a Part D plan are usually redundant for a TFL family; the open question is whether an MA-only Advantage plan’s extras are worth it, and whether its network reaches Wayne Memorial and the ECU Health or Triangle specialists Goldsboro residents are referred to.

Seymour Johnson has a clinic, not a hospital: the 4th Medical Group’s Koritz Clinic serves enrolled retirees and dependents, and inpatient care is civilian, at Wayne Memorial Hospital in Goldsboro. Veterans without TFL use the Durham VA’s eastern clinics, and for them the Part B timing rule is the one that matters.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. Moving into North Carolina, or a plan leaving your county, opens a Special Enrollment Period.

Questions from Seymour Johnson AFB (Goldsboro) families

Frequently asked questions

I retired from the Air Force with TRICARE For Life. Do I need anything besides Medicare A and B?

Usually not. TFL fills Medicare’s gaps and its pharmacy is creditable. An MA-only Advantage plan can add dental or vision; we check whether one is worth it and whether its network reaches your doctors.

Can I get hospital care on base after 65?

No. Seymour Johnson’s 4th Medical Group runs an outpatient clinic that serves enrolled retirees; hospital care is at Wayne Memorial or a referral hospital, where TFL pays after Medicare.

I use the VA and do not have TRICARE. Do I need Part B?

In most cases, yes. VA care is not creditable for Part B; delaying it triggers a permanent penalty.

Medicare near Camp Lejeune (Jacksonville)

Why the Medicare choice is different around Camp Lejeune

Onslow, Carteret and Craven counties are Marine Corps retiree country: Camp Lejeune, New River and Cherry Point all retire people who stay for the coast. Most reach 65 with TRICARE For Life, which requires Medicare Parts A and B, pays secondary to Medicare and carries creditable pharmacy coverage. For a TFL family, a Medigap policy and a Part D plan are usually redundant; the live question is whether an MA-only Advantage plan’s dental, vision and hearing extras are worth having.

Naval Medical Center Camp Lejeune serves more than 150,000 active-duty, retiree and family beneficiaries and sees retirees on a space-available basis; Medicare does not pay there. The Fayetteville Coastal VA’s Jacksonville clinic serves veterans without TFL, and for them the Part B timing rule is the one that matters. Veterans covered by the Camp Lejeune water-contamination provisions have VA care for the listed conditions; that is separate from, and does not replace, Medicare.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. Moving into North Carolina, or a plan leaving your county, opens a Special Enrollment Period.

Questions from Camp Lejeune (Jacksonville) families

Frequently asked questions

I retired from the Marine Corps with TRICARE For Life. Which Medicare plan should I pick?

For most TFL families, Original Medicare Parts A and B with TFL is complete coverage. An MA-only Advantage plan can add dental, vision and hearing without duplicating your pharmacy benefit; we check whether one is worth it for you.

Does Medicare pay at Naval Medical Center Camp Lejeune?

No. The Naval Medical Center sees retirees on a space-available basis under TRICARE rules. Most Lejeune retirees pair TFL with civilian care at Onslow Memorial, CarolinaEast or Carteret Health Care.

I use the VA clinic in Jacksonville and do not have TRICARE. Do I need Part B?

In most cases, yes. VA care is not creditable for Part B; delaying it triggers a permanent penalty and leaves you uncovered at a non-VA hospital.

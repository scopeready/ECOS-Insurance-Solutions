Dual Special Needs Plans in Storey County, Nevada — Who Qualifies and What to Check

If you have both Medicare and Medicaid in Storey County, a D-SNP can wrap your coverage into one plan. Here is how it works locally — including every city and ZIP code in the county.

Storey County is Nevada's second-smallest by area and has no hospital of its own. Almost every resident receives care in another county — Carson City or the Reno–Sparks area. That makes the useful question here an unusual one: not who is in network in Storey County, but whether a Dual Special Needs Plan works well in the two counties where you will actually be seen.

A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP) is a Medicare Advantage plan for people who have both Medicare and Medicaid. It combines Part A, Part B and Part D prescription coverage in one plan, usually at a $0 premium, because Medicaid already covers most Medicare cost-sharing. Nevada runs a coordination-only D-SNP program, and whether you qualify in Storey County depends on which dual eligibility category you fall into. Eligibility categories, how Nevada structures its program, and the enrollment windows are covered in full on the Nevada D-SNP guide →.

Getting care in Storey County

There is no hospital in Storey County. Residents use Carson Tahoe Regional Medical Center in Carson City or the Reno–Sparks hospitals, including Renown, Northern Nevada Medical Center and Saint Mary's.

Evaluate plans on their Carson City and Washoe County networks rather than on anything local. Storey County residents have good access compared with most counties in this service area — the drive is short — but the network match still has to be right.

Before You Enroll

Three things to check in Storey County

These are the questions that actually change the answer here.

  • Judge the plan on Carson City and Reno–Sparks. There is no hospital in Storey County, so the networks that matter are the ones in the counties where you will be seen.
  • Confirm your county if you have a Sparks address. Lockwood and the Tahoe-Reno Industrial Center use 89434 but sit in Storey County, and eligibility follows the county.
  • Check local pharmacy access. Virginia City is small. Confirm which pharmacies are in network nearby, or whether mail-order is the better route.

More plan choice than these counties are used to

Rural Nevada has historically been thin ground for Medicare Advantage. Counties in this part of the state have long had far fewer plans than Clark or Washoe — sometimes only a handful, sometimes nothing worth switching for. That picture has changed for 2026, and Storey County is one of the counties where it is worth a fresh look.

If you priced Medicare Advantage in Storey County a few years ago and concluded there was nothing here for you, that conclusion may simply be out of date. This is the part we would rather walk through with you directly — plan availability is defined county by county and changes every year, so the only answer that means anything is the one for your ZIP code, this plan year.

And Medicaid changed too, on January 1, 2026

Storey County moved into Medicaid managed care on January 1, 2026. Since essentially all Storey County care happens in Carson City or the Reno–Sparks area, what matters is your new Medicaid plan's network in those two places rather than anything inside the county line.

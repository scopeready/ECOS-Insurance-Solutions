Dual Special Needs Plans in Mineral County, Nevada — Who Qualifies and What to Check

If you have both Medicare and Medicaid in Mineral County, a D-SNP can wrap your coverage into one plan. Here is how it works locally — including every city and ZIP code in the county.

Mineral County runs from Walker Lake down through Hawthorne and out along US Highway 95, with roughly 4,500 residents across about 3,757 square miles. It has one hospital, and specialty care generally means a trip to Reno — about 138 miles northwest. For someone with both Medicare and Medicaid, that drive is the thing a Dual Special Needs Plan most needs to help with.

A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP) is a Medicare Advantage plan for people who have both Medicare and Medicaid. It combines Part A, Part B and Part D prescription coverage in one plan, usually at a $0 premium, because Medicaid already covers most Medicare cost-sharing. Nevada runs a coordination-only D-SNP program, and whether you qualify in Mineral County depends on which dual eligibility category you fall into. Eligibility categories, how Nevada structures its program, and the enrollment windows are covered in full on the Nevada D-SNP guide →.

Getting care in Mineral County

Mt. Grant General Hospital in Hawthorne is a county-owned district hospital serving roughly 4,500 residents, about 138 miles southeast of Reno. Schurz is served by the Walker River Paiute Tribe's health clinic.

Mt. Grant is the only hospital for a wide area, so check its network status first. Then ask the second question that actually matters here: how does the plan pay for the specialty care you will end up receiving in Washoe County?

Mineral County stretches from Walker Lake in the north, through Hawthorne — home to the Hawthorne Army Depot — and south along US-95 toward Esmeralda County.

Before You Enroll

Three things to check in Mineral County

These are the questions that actually change the answer here.

  • Confirm Mt. Grant General Hospital. It is the only hospital for a wide area and the first thing to verify in Mineral County.
  • Ask what happens for Reno referrals. Roughly 138 miles separates Hawthorne from Reno. Check both the Washoe County network and the transportation benefit.
  • If you are in Schurz, ask about tribal health coordination. The Walker River Paiute Tribe operates a clinic there, and plans differ in how they coordinate with tribal and IHS facilities.

More plan choice than these counties are used to

Rural Nevada has historically been thin ground for Medicare Advantage. Counties in this part of the state have long had far fewer plans than Clark or Washoe — sometimes only a handful, sometimes nothing worth switching for. That picture has changed for 2026, and Mineral County is one of the counties where it is worth a fresh look.

If you priced Medicare Advantage in Mineral County a few years ago and concluded there was nothing here for you, that conclusion may simply be out of date. This is the part we would rather walk through with you directly — plan availability is defined county by county and changes every year, so the only answer that means anything is the one for your ZIP code, this plan year.

And Medicaid changed too, on January 1, 2026

Mineral County moved out of fee-for-service Medicaid into managed care on January 1, 2026. For Hawthorne residents the useful question is how the new plan handles the Reno referrals that Mt. Grant cannot take, including the transportation to get there.

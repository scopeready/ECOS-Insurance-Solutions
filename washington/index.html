Washington is different

Three things about Medicare in Washington that the national websites gloss over

Washington has 39 counties, a mountain range down the middle that splits its health care in two, a state law on Medigap that most of the country would envy, and one of the largest military-retiree populations on the West Coast. Start with what actually applies where you live.

You can change Medigap plans any time

Washington law lets anyone who already holds a Medigap policy move to another Medigap plan at any point in the year, with no health questions. A Plan A holder can only move to another Plan A, and the rule is for existing Medigap policyholders, not for leaving an Advantage plan — but it changes how you shop, and how much a rate increase should worry you.

Two Washingtons, one mountain range

West of the Cascades the menu is deep and the question is which system your doctors belong to: UW Medicine, Providence Swedish, MultiCare, Virginia Mason Franciscan or Kaiser Permanente Washington. East of the passes, and along the coast, the menu is short and the nearest specialist may be two hours away, which is where a supplement’s any-provider access earns its premium.

A military state on Medicare

Joint Base Lewis-McChord, Naval Base Kitsap and Fairchild Air Force Base each anchor a large retiree community, and VA Puget Sound alone serves more than 125,000 veterans. TRICARE For Life and VA care each work with Medicare differently, and the Part B timing mistake is expensive and permanent.

Questions Washingtonians ask

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to work with ECOS Medicare Solutions?

There is no cost to you. Independent Medicare agents are paid by the insurance carriers when you enroll, so our help comparing plans, answering questions, and reviewing your coverage each year is free. Your plan premium is the same whether you enroll with our help or on your own.

Can I really change my Medigap plan any time in Washington?

If you already have a Medigap policy, yes. Washington law lets you switch to another Medigap plan at any time of year without medical underwriting; the one limit is that a Plan A holder can only move to another Plan A. It does not apply to a move from Medicare Advantage into Medigap, which relies on your six-month open enrollment, a federal guaranteed-issue right, or underwriting. Our switching guide walks through it.

When can I enroll in or change my Medicare plan in Washington?

Most people first enroll during their Initial Enrollment Period, the seven months around their 65th birthday. After that, the Annual Election Period runs October 15 to December 7 each year, and the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period runs January 1 to March 31. Moving counties, losing a plan, or qualifying for Apple Health opens a Special Enrollment Period, and Medigap policyholders can change plans any time.

Is UW Medicine in my Medicare Advantage plan?

It depends on the plan and the year. UW Medicine accepts Original Medicare, and therefore any Medigap policy. For 2026 it has said it will not be contracted with Humana, PacificSource or Wellpoint plans, and its list of contracted plans changes each fall. If UW Medicine, Fred Hutch, Providence Swedish, Virginia Mason or a specific system is your care, we confirm the plan’s status in writing before you enroll.

I have VA or TRICARE benefits. Do I still need Medicare?

Often, yes. VA health care and Medicare do not coordinate with each other, and TRICARE For Life requires you to have Medicare Parts A and B. Enrolling in Part B on time matters even with VA care, because VA medical coverage is not creditable for Part B and the late penalty lasts for life. Our Veterans page explains how these benefits fit together.

Do you offer every Medicare plan available in my area?

No — and we will always be upfront about that. We represent a number of insurance organizations and products in Washington, not all of them. The easiest next step is to call us at (702) 706-6564 and we will walk through what fits you. To compare every option on your own, Medicare.gov, 1-800-MEDICARE, and Washington SHIBA (800-562-6900) have the complete list.

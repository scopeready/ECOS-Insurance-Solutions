Medicare near Joint Base Charleston

Why the Medicare choice is different for a Joint Base Charleston family

Charleston, Berkeley and Dorchester counties hold one of the largest military-retiree communities in the Southeast, and most of them reach 65 with TRICARE For Life waiting. TFL requires Medicare Parts A and B and then pays secondary to Medicare, so a Medigap policy is usually unnecessary; its pharmacy is creditable, so a Part D plan usually is too. What is left to decide is whether an MA-only Advantage plan adds dental, vision or hearing worth having — without duplicating the drug coverage you already have.

Veterans without TRICARE use the Ralph H. Johnson VA Medical Center on Bee Street and its clinics in North Charleston and Goose Creek, and here the Part B timing question is the one that matters: VA care is not creditable for Part B, so delaying Part B because you have the VA triggers a lifelong penalty. The base clinic and Naval Health Clinic Charleston continue to see retirees on a space-available basis; neither is a substitute for Medicare.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. Moving to South Carolina, losing a plan, or a FEMA hurricane declaration each open a Special Enrollment Period.

Questions from Joint Base Charleston families

Frequently asked questions

I retired from the Air Force with TRICARE For Life. Do I need a Medigap policy or Part D?

Usually neither. TFL pays secondary to Medicare and its pharmacy is creditable drug coverage. An MA-only Advantage plan can add dental or vision without duplicating your drug benefit; we check whether one is worth it for you.

I use the Charleston VA. Do I still need Part B?

In most cases, yes. VA medical care is not creditable coverage for Part B, so delaying it can trigger a permanent penalty and leaves you without coverage at a non-VA hospital. Enroll on time.

Can I keep using the base clinic after 65?

Retirees can be seen at the base and Navy clinics on a space-available basis under TRICARE rules; Medicare does not pay there. Most Joint Base Charleston retirees pair TFL with civilian care at Trident, MUSC or Roper St. Francis.

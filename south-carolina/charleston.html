Medicare in Charleston

What to know before you compare plans in Charleston County

Charleston County offers a wide Medicare Advantage menu, every Medigap carrier licensed in South Carolina and the full Part D lineup. The catch is that Charleston’s care is organized into systems that contract separately: MUSC Health downtown and its regional hospitals, Roper St. Francis Healthcare with Roper Hospital and Bon Secours St. Francis in West Ashley, Trident Health in North Charleston and Summerville, and Novant Health East Cooper across the Ravenel Bridge. A plan that includes MUSC may exclude Roper, or the reverse.

Charleston is also a Navy and Air Force retirement town, and a landing place for retirees from the Northeast who arrive already on Medicare. We compare what is actually offered at your ZIP code, confirm your doctors and pharmacy, and set Advantage beside a supplement so you can see the trade-off plainly.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. Moving to South Carolina, losing a plan, or a FEMA hurricane declaration each open a Special Enrollment Period.

Questions from Charleston

Frequently asked questions

Which Medicare plans are available in Charleston?

Charleston County typically has one of the widest choices in South Carolina: a broad Medicare Advantage menu, every Medigap carrier in the state, and all standalone Part D plans. Availability is set by ZIP code and changes each plan year; we compare what is offered at your address.

Will my MUSC or Roper St. Francis doctors take a Medicare Advantage plan?

It depends on the specific plan’s network, and the contracts change every year. Before you enroll we confirm your providers at MUSC, Roper, Bon Secours St. Francis, Trident or East Cooper are in-network and your prescriptions are on the formulary, so there are no surprises in January.

How much does your help cost?

Nothing. Independent agents are paid by the carriers when you enroll, so comparing plans and reviewing your coverage each year is free to you.
